Imagine an auction in a room. Someone holds up a toy, and kids shout prices. "Ten rupees!" "Twenty!" The highest shout when time runs out wins the toy.

One person writes the shouts down in a notebook, so everyone knows who's winning. Fine — until that person falls asleep. Now nobody knows who won, and the last few shouts are lost forever.

So we use three people with three notebooks. Now if one falls asleep, the other two still know. But a new problem appears: they might write different things. One hears "twenty," another hears "thirty." Now who won?

Raft is the rulebook for keeping the three notebooks the same. The rules are simple:

One of the three is the boss. Only the boss writes down new shouts.
When the boss hears a shout, it tells the other two: "write this down."
A shout only really counts once two of the three have it in their notebook. Then the boss tells the kid: "yes, your bid is in."
The boss keeps saying "I'm still awake, I'm still awake." If the other two stop hearing that, they know the boss fell asleep. They vote and pick a new boss.

Here's the clever bit, the one thing worth understanding: because every counted shout was written in two of three notebooks, and a new boss needs two of three votes to win — the new boss is guaranteed to already have every shout that counted. Nothing that was promised can be lost. That's the whole trick.

gRPC is just how they talk to each other — a strict little language where every sentence has an agreed shape, so nobody can mishear.

The LLM server is a helpful friend in the corner. It answers "how does bidding work?" and writes nice descriptions of the toys. It never touches the notebooks. It's a helper, not a record-keeper.

And the three things we just added are your proof:

The dashboard is a window into all three notebooks at once, so you can watch the boss fall asleep and a new one get elected.
The checker is a referee holding every receipt: "you promised these twenty bids — show me all twenty are still there."
The chaos harness is a mischief-maker who knocks people asleep at random times — but with a magic dice that remembers. Say "dice number 42" and it does the exact same mischief again, so you can watch the bug happen a second time instead of hoping it comes back.
